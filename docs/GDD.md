# Navbatchi 112 / Диспетчер 112 / Dispatch 112 — Game Design Document

## 1. Pitch
A **real-time emergency-dispatch simulator in the terminal**. Calls scroll in, the ASCII city map is alive with
ambulances, fire engines and patrol cars, and you have seconds to read each call, decide what it really is, and send
the right unit — or dismiss it as a prank. Five night shifts. A quiet pattern hides in the fire calls.

| | |
|---|---|
| Genre | Real-time management / triage simulation with a light investigation story |
| Platform | Native terminal app (TUI), macOS / Linux / Windows |
| Tech | Rust, `ratatui` + `crossterm`; pure, tick-based simulation core with no terminal code; `cargo test` |
| Audience | 14+, fans of fast management games (dispatch, air-traffic-style); one shift ≈ 6–8 real minutes, full week ≈ 40 min |
| Languages | Uzbek (Latin), Russian, English |
| Difficulty | Rookie / Dispatcher (default) / Veteran — call rate, unit count and escalation speed |

## 2. Story
**Setting:** the fictional city of **Yangiobod**, a single week of night shifts (Monday–Friday, 22:00–06:00).
**You:** a rookie dispatcher at the city's unified 112 centre.
**Characters:** **Zuhra-opa** (veteran mentor, retires on Friday, gives tips between shifts and comments on your grades);
**Captain Akmal** (fire brigade chief, gruff); **Inspector Dilshod** (police, asks for your notes on suspicious calls);
recurring callers (a lonely pensioner who calls every night, a pranking teenager, a night-shift baker).

**Plot thread — the arsonist:** each night 1–3 fire calls are part of a pattern (same district, same "smell of petrol",
always a warehouse / kiosk / garage, a caller with the same phrase). Flag suspicious calls (`F`). Between shifts Inspector
Dilshod reviews your flags. With ≥ 4 correct flags by Thursday (and no more false flags than correct ones) he sets a
trap; on Friday the arsonist first sets a diversion fire in the Bazaar, then makes his last call from the Industrial Zone —
dispatching police + fire to the right place catches him. (See §7 for the exact rules.)

**Endings (5)**
| # | Ending | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Golden Headset** (best) | all 5 shifts graded B or better and the arsonist caught |
| E2 | **Steady Hand** | week completed, arsonist not caught |
| E3 | **Night Detective** | arsonist caught but one or more shifts graded C/D |
| E4 | **Badge Returned** (lose) | city trust falls to 0 in any shift — you are fired |
| E5 | **Burnout** (lose) | stress reaches 100 — you walk out mid-shift |

## 3. Simulation
- **Time:** 1 tick = 1 game minute; real-time with pause (Space) and speed ×1/×2/×4. A shift = 480 ticks.
- **Map:** 58×18 character grid (fits a 100×30 terminal next to the other panes) with roads, a river with four bridges,
  six districts, hospital (H), fire station (F), police station (P); units move along roads (BFS path, speed in cells per
  tick, sirens faster). Map is drawn with box characters and colour.
- **Calls:** generated by a seeded scheduler from 69 call templates × variants (3 languages). Each call has a hidden true
  type (medical / fire / police / prank), severity 1–3, location, and a text that may be misleading
  ("my grandfather fell and there is smoke" = fire + medical).
- **Dispatching:** select a call → choose unit(s) → Enter. Units travel, work on scene for a template-defined time,
  then return to base. Wrong unit type = no resolution and wasted time; a call left in the queue escalates severity
  over time; severity-3 calls not reached in time cost lives.
- **Prank calls:** dismiss with `D`; dismissing a real call is a big penalty; dispatching to a prank wastes a unit.
- **Meters:** City trust 0–100 (start 60; +resolved, −lives lost, −mistakes), Stress 0–100 (+queue length, +losses,
  −calm periods, −coffee break once per shift which pauses incoming calls for 15 min but costs trust).
- **Grades:** each shift scores lives saved, average response time vs. severity, prank accuracy → S/A/B/C/D.
  Between shifts: grade screen, Zuhra-opa's comment, Inspector's review, and an upgrade choice (extra ambulance,
  faster fire engine, call-screening training that reveals prank hints, …).

## 4. Progression & difficulty
Monday is gentle (tutorial overlays from Zuhra-opa); call rate and multi-unit incidents grow each night; Wednesday adds
a storm (slower roads), Thursday a stadium event (police-heavy), Friday the arsonist finale. Difficulty scales call rate,
escalation speed and starting units.

## 5. UI & controls
Panes: call queue (left), selected call text + details (bottom), map (centre), units list (right), status bar (time,
trust, stress, shift, speed) plus a radio log (bottom right). Keys: ↑/↓ select call · Tab switch pane · 1–9 choose unit ·
Enter dispatch · Esc clear picked units · D dismiss · F flag as suspicious · R recall unit(s) · C coffee break ·
Space pause · +/− speed · L language · ? help · Q quit (with confirm).
Works in a 100×30 terminal; shows a "please enlarge the terminal" message if smaller.

## 6. Testing
- Pure engine tests: pathfinding, unit travel times, escalation, scoring/grades, prank handling, ending selection.
- Headless whole-week simulations with a scripted "good" bot (should finish the week, grades ≥ B on Rookie) and a
  "do nothing" bot (must lose by trust collapse); determinism test (same seed → same result).
- i18n completeness test (every key present in uz/ru/en with matching placeholders).
- `--autoplay` flag runs the bot in the real TUI for a demo; `--headless` prints a text summary.

## 7. Implemented rules and numbers (v1.0)
These are the values the shipped build uses (tuned with the headless balance runs; see README).

**Time.** 1 tick = 850 ms at ×1, so a 480-tick shift lasts ≈ 6.8 real minutes (≈ 3.4 at ×2, ≈ 1.7 at ×4). Tutorial tips
and the help/quit overlays pause the clock.

**Difficulty.**
| | Rookie | Dispatcher | Veteran |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean minutes between ordinary calls (Monday) | 23 | 16 | 14 |
| Extra call rate per night | +10 % | +12 % | +14 % |
| Minutes for an unattended call to rise one severity level | 34 | 26 | 22 |
| Minutes a critical (severity 3) call survives without help | 30 | 22 | 19 |
| Starting fleet (ambulances / fire engines / patrol cars) | 3 / 2 / 2 | 3 / 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 / 2 |
| Trust lost per failed call | 8 | 10 | 10 |
| Stress gain multiplier | 0.8 | 1.0 | 1.2 |
| Share of pranks among ordinary calls | 12 % | 14 % | 16 % |

**Units.** Siren speed (cells/tick): ambulance 2.0, fire engine 1.6, patrol car 2.4; returning 1.2. On-scene work:
template time × (1 + 0.2 per severity above 1). Units can be re-tasked while returning, not while committed (use R to
recall). At most 9 vehicles (keys 1–9).

**Calls and resolution.** Each call has hidden needs (e.g. 1 ambulance + 1 police) and what the caller's words suggest.
A call is resolved when every needed service has finished its work. A unit of the wrong service looks around for 2
minutes, radios back what is really needed (the true needs are then shown to the player) and leaves (−1 trust).
Escalation is frozen while every needed service is on scene. A severity-3 call that is not covered by its deadline fails
(−trust, +7 stress, "lives lost" if an ambulance was needed). Real callers left with nobody sent for more than 12 minutes
slowly erode trust (−0.05 per caller per minute).

**Pranks and non-emergencies.** Dismiss (D): +0.5 trust. A unit sent to a prank finds nothing (−1.5 trust). Ignored
pranks hang up after 30–40 minutes (no trust change, but it counts against prank handling). Dismissing a real call: the
caller calls back 10 minutes later one severity level worse, −12 trust.

**Meters.** Trust starts at 60 and carries over between nights; resolving a call gives +0.5 × severity. Stress rises
with the number of open calls and of unanswered calls, falls when the queue is quiet, and 40 % of it carries into the
next night. Coffee break (C, once per shift): −30 stress, −4 trust, and for 15 minutes Zuhra-opa takes ordinary calls
(story calls wait until the break ends).

**Score and grades.** Lives & property 50 × (resolved share)² (calls handed to the morning shift count half), response
30 × average response quality (full marks within 10 / 16 / 26 minutes for severity 3 / 2 / 1, zero at three times
that), prank handling 20, minus wrong-service dispatches. S ≥ 92, A ≥ 84, B ≥ 72, C ≥ 56, D below.

**Nights.** Monday: tutorial tips, gentler first two hours, an obvious prank and a two-service crash early on.
Wednesday: storm — units 15 % slower, the Bazaar bridge is flooded (closed), storm incidents (fallen trees, power lines,
flooded underpass). Thursday: derby — busy first hours, stadium fights, crowd crush, pickpockets. Friday: the finale.

**Recurring callers.** Karim-bobo, a lonely pensioner, calls to chat on Monday–Wednesday and Friday (dismiss) — but on
Thursday he has real chest pain. Jasur, a teenage prankster, prank-calls Monday–Thursday; on Friday his call about a fire
at the school gym is real. Botir the baker reports a gas smell (Mon), a break-in (Tue), a tree on his van (Wed), and on
Thursday sees a man in a grey jacket with a petrol can in the Industrial Zone — that call also counts as a pattern call.

**The arsonist.** Pattern calls: Monday 1, Tuesday 2, Wednesday and Thursday 2–3 each (+ Botir's Thursday sighting).
They are fires at a warehouse, kiosk or garage in the Industrial Zone, with a smell of petrol and the same calm voice
ending with the same phrase ("Hurry — or you'll be late again"). Decoys share some features (a transformer fire in the
Industrial Zone, petrol at a filling station, a garage fire elsewhere). After each of Monday–Thursday the inspector
reviews the night's flags; the trap is set if, by the end of Thursday, correct flags ≥ 4 and false flags ≤ correct
flags. Friday with the trap: a diversion fire in the Bazaar and, four minutes later, the finale call (needs fire +
police). Resolving it (both services on scene and done) catches the arsonist. Without the trap, Friday's arson call is
an ordinary fire and he cannot be caught.

**Between shifts.** Grade screen → Zuhra-opa's comment (+ Captain Akmal's complaint after several wrong-service
dispatches; her farewell on Friday) → Inspector Dilshod's review → one of three upgrades: extra ambulance, railway
ambulance post (an ambulance based south of the river), extra fire engine, extra patrol car, new fire engines (+0.5
speed), call-screening training (a hint line under each call), green wave (+15 % speed), stress training (−25 % stress
gain), rapid triage (ambulance work −20 %). Progress is saved after each shift to `~/.dispatch112/progress.json`
(`DISPATCH112_HOME` overrides the folder).
